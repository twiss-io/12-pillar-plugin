# The 12-Pillar Life Flywheel

> A life is one system, not twelve separate departments. Each pillar produces something the next pillar runs on. When an area is stuck, the cause is usually upstream of it — fix that first.

This document is the reference the AI uses to understand, organise, and coach a person's life. Read it fully before running any command in this plugin.

## 1. The core idea

Most people manage life in silos: a career plan here, a fitness plan there, finances somewhere else. The 12-Pillar Life Flywheel treats a life as a chain of twelve pillars where **each pillar gives something (its "currency") to the next**:

> Faith gives direction → Identity gives clarity → Time gives allocation → Health gives capacity → Development gives skill → Relationships give access → Career gives credibility → Brand gives opportunity → Opportunities become finance → Finance funds lifestyle → Lifestyle feeds legacy → Legacy loops back to faith.

Three consequences follow, and they drive everything the AI does with this framework:

1. **You can't shortcut downstream.** Wealth, opportunity, and reputation are outputs. They depend on pillars further up the chain. Optimising a downstream pillar while an upstream one is broken produces effort without results.
2. **Diagnose upstream.** When someone reports a problem in one pillar, look one or two pillars earlier in the chain for the real cause.
3. **Momentum spreads.** Because the pillars are connected, getting one moving pulls the neighbouring pillars along. You don't need to fix all twelve at once. Start where it hurts most and let the flywheel carry the rest.

## 2. The twelve pillars

Each entry gives the pillar's purpose, its currency (what it gives the next pillar), what lives inside it, how to tell if it's healthy or stalled, and good opening questions.

### 1 · Faith & Inner Compass — gives **Direction**
- **Purpose:** what the person believes, what they're anchored to, and why they do what they do. Religious or not — this is the inner life.
- **Inside:** beliefs and values, spiritual or reflective practice (prayer, meditation, journaling, stillness), personal reviews, the "why" behind big decisions.
- **Healthy:** decisions feel anchored; the person can say why they're doing what they're doing.
- **Stalled:** drifting, reactive, busy without a sense of purpose; big decisions made by default.
- **Ask:** "What do you believe your life is for?" · "When did you last stop and reflect on direction?"

### 2 · Identity & Life Vision — gives **Clarity**
- **Purpose:** who the person is and the life they're building toward.
- **Inside:** self-understanding, personal history, strengths, lifelong goals, the vision for the built life, written as **declarations** — present-tense statements of the life as if it already exists ("I am…", "I have…").
- **Healthy:** the person can describe the life they want concretely, and their choices line up with it.
- **Stalled:** vague goals, constantly comparing, saying yes to everything.
- **Ask:** "Describe the life you're building, in the present tense." · "What are you, at your best?"

### 3 · Time & Energy — gives **Allocation**
- **Purpose:** turning direction and clarity into a plan for the hours in a week.
- **Inside:** the weekly time budget, routines, calendar structure, seasons (periods of deliberate intensity and their end dates), energy patterns.
- **Healthy:** important things get scheduled before urgent things; there is time for the non-negotiables.
- **Stalled:** "no time" for health, family, or reflection; everything feels urgent; days blur together.
- **Ask:** "Where did your hours actually go last week?" · "What did you want time for but didn't get?"
- **Note:** "No time" is almost always an allocation problem, not a shortage. A week has 168 hours.

### 4 · Health & Longevity — gives **Capacity**
- **Purpose:** the body and mind that everything else runs on.
- **Inside:** sleep, exercise, nutrition, medical history, mental health, recovery, tracking (weight, bloodwork, sleep data).
- **Healthy:** enough energy to do the work and enjoy life; sleep is protected.
- **Stalled:** tired, sick often, running on caffeine, skipping sleep to get more done.
- **Ask:** "How many hours do you really sleep?" · "What's the one health habit you keep meaning to start?"

### 5 · Personal Development — gives **Skill**
- **Purpose:** growing what the person is capable of.
- **Inside:** education history, certifications, a skills inventory, reading and learning notes, "what to learn next". A useful heuristic: find what the person does faster and more easily than others (often something they find boring), then add the skills just upstream and downstream of it.
- **Healthy:** skills are compounding in a direction that serves the vision.
- **Stalled:** random courses, no clear next skill, skills going stale.
- **Ask:** "What do people always ask you for help with?" · "What skill would change the most if you had it?"

### 6 · Relationships — gives **Access**
- **Purpose:** family, partner, friends, mentors, and the wider network. Most opportunities arrive through people.
- **Inside:** a page per important person (what they care about, family, work, interests, what they're working toward, important dates, last contact), who belongs in which circle, how often each relationship should be touched.
- **Healthy:** key relationships are maintained on purpose, not by accident; there's at least one mentor.
- **Stalled:** only reaching out when something is needed; forgotten birthdays; important people neglected.
- **Ask:** "Who would show up for you no matter what?" · "Who haven't you spoken to in too long?"
- **Note:** closeness is measured by trust and openness, not years known.

### 7 · Career & Track Record — gives **Credibility**
- **Purpose:** the person's professional history and the evidence of what they've done.
- **Inside:** a living CV, roles and responsibilities, wins, references and testimonials, a private record of everything (fuller than any public profile).
- **Healthy:** wins are recorded when they happen, with proof; the person can show what they're capable of.
- **Stalled:** achievements forgotten, nothing written down, CV only updated when job-hunting.
- **Ask:** "What's the best piece of work you've done?" · "Who could vouch for it?"
- **Note:** capture the testimonial at the moment of the win. It's much harder to reconstruct later.

### 8 · Personal Brand & Authority — gives **Opportunity**
- **Purpose:** what the outside world knows and believes about the person.
- **Inside:** positioning (what they want to be known for), chosen channels, content, public profiles, speaking, reputation.
- **Healthy:** the right people know what the person is great at, so opportunities come to them.
- **Stalled:** invisible outside a small circle, or known for the wrong thing.
- **Ask:** "What do you want to be known for?" · "If someone searched for you, what would they find?"

### 9 · Work, Business & Opportunities — becomes **Finance**
- **Purpose:** the deals, projects, jobs, and ventures where effort turns into money.
- **Inside:** a page per business, project, or opportunity; the criteria for which opportunities to say yes to; pipeline and status.
- **Healthy:** there are clear rules for which opportunities to take, and the pipeline is visible.
- **Stalled:** chasing everything, or nothing in the pipeline.
- **Ask:** "What are you working on right now?" · "What kind of opportunity do you say no to?"
- **Note:** businesses the person runs get their own folder (see `folder-design.md`).

### 10 · Finance & Wealth — funds **Lifestyle**
- **Purpose:** money in, money out, and what it's building.
- **Inside:** income, expenses, net worth, savings, investments. A useful structure is four tiers: **necessities** (to survive) → **commitments** (fixed obligations like loans, insurance, family support) → **lifestyle** → **investment**. The first two tiers together are the person's **base number** — the monthly amount they must cover. Knowing it tells you how much risk they can take.
- **Healthy:** the person knows their base number and cash flow; money is building toward something.
- **Stalled:** no idea where money goes; taking risks without knowing the floor.
- **Ask:** "What's the minimum you need each month?" · "What's your money building toward?"

### 11 · Lifestyle & Experiences — feeds **Legacy**
- **Purpose:** what the person wants to enjoy — experiences, things, places, how they live.
- **Inside:** travel, hobbies, purchases, experiences, each with a rough budget and the point in the person's path when it makes sense.
- **Healthy:** enjoyment is planned and earned, not used to escape.
- **Stalled:** either all work with nothing to enjoy, or spending ahead of what the finances support.
- **Ask:** "What would you do if you had a free month?" · "What are you looking forward to?"

### 12 · Legacy & Stewardship — loops back to **Faith**
- **Purpose:** what the person leaves behind — for family, community, and the people they've shaped.
- **Inside:** values to pass on, giving, mentoring others, estate and succession, the projected outcome of their life.
- **Healthy:** the person knows what they want to leave, and today's choices point that way.
- **Stalled:** never considered; living only for the next quarter.
- **Ask:** "What do you want people to say about you at your funeral?" · "Who are you building this for?"
- **Note:** thinking about legacy sends you back to pillar 1 — what you believe and why you're here — and the flywheel starts its next turn at a deeper level.

## 3. How the AI uses this framework

1. **Enter where it hurts.** Never force a full twelve-pillar interview. Ask which area the person most wants fixed and start there. Offer the next pillar once there's momentum.
2. **Diagnose upstream.** When a problem shows up in one pillar, check the one or two pillars before it first, and name the chain out loud so the person learns the logic. Examples: no opportunities → check brand, relationships, career. Weak execution → check health and time. Money stress → check opportunities and the base number.
3. **Check the currency.** For any pillar under discussion, ask: "Is this pillar actually giving the next one what it should?" A pillar can look busy and still produce nothing. A packed calendar that doesn't protect what matters isn't giving allocation.
4. **Empty pillars are fine.** An empty pillar isn't a failure. It just isn't running yet. Never invent content to fill it. It comes alive through capture: every time the person shares something, file it into each pillar it touches.
5. **Never silo.** In any plan or review, connect at least one upstream and one downstream pillar so the person sees their life as one system.
6. **Offer the self-assessment.** Periodically invite the person to score each pillar 1–10, find the weakest, and start there (`/12-pillars:pillars`).
7. **Facts over stories.** Hold an honest standard: what actually happened and how the person actually feels. If an answer sounds polished, gently ask for the unpolished version.

## 4. Where the pillars live in the folder

In this plugin, the pillars are **wiki pages**, not twelve folders. Each pillar has one hub page at `life/wiki/pillars/NN-<name>.md` that summarises where that pillar stands and links to the detail pages (people, topics, notes, sources) that feed it. One captured note can link from several pillar hubs at once. See `llm-wiki.md` for how pages are written and kept up to date.
